\chapter{Cavity microwave-photon counting}
\label{ch:microwave-absorption}

A high-$Q$ microwave cavity tuned to the Compton frequency
$\omega_\chi=m_\chi c^2/\hbar$ of a candidate bosonic dark-matter
particle accumulates a coherent intracavity photon population that
a transmon, operated as a single-microwave-photon counter, reads
out one quantum at a time. This chapter develops the cavity-mode
toolbox, surveys the cQED single-microwave-photon detectors that
implement the readout, works through the dispersive-shift and
four-wave-mixing photon-counting schemes in detail, and translates
the resulting performance into dark-photon and axion sensitivity.

% --------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Electromagnetic modes in a resonant cavity}
\label{sec:cavity-modes}

\begin{phenomenon}
A closed conducting cavity supports a discrete set of resonant
electromagnetic modes, each carrying a fixed spatial profile and
a fixed quality factor. A bosonic dark-matter field at the same
frequency drives the resonant mode coherently, producing an
intracavity photon population whose magnitude is proportional to
$|\bm A_{\rm DM}|^2$, the square of the dark-matter field
amplitude.
\end{phenomenon}

\begin{statement}[Helmholtz cavity modes~\cite{pozar2011}]
\label{stmt:cavity-modes}
The vector potential of a free electromagnetic mode in a vacuum
cavity bounded by a perfect conductor satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:helmholtz-cavity}
\bigl(\nabla^2 + k^2\bigr)\,\bm A(\bm r) \;=\; 0,
\qquad k=\omega/c,
\end{equation}
with the conductor boundary condition $\hat n\times\bm E = 0$ on
each cavity wall. The discrete eigenvalues
$k_{nml}=\omega_{nml}/c$ label TE and TM modes. For a rectangular
cavity of dimensions $a\times b\times d$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rect-cavity-modes}
\omega_{nml} \;=\; c\sqrt{\Bigl(\frac{n\pi}{a}\Bigr)^2 + \Bigl(\frac{m\pi}{b}\Bigr)^2 + \Bigl(\frac{l\pi}{d}\Bigr)^2}.
\end{equation}
For a cylindrical cavity of radius $R$ and height $L$ the modes
are labelled by $(n,m,l)$ with the radial profile of the $m$-th
mode given by Bessel functions $J_m(k_{nm}\rho)$. Each mode has
a loaded quality factor $Q_L$ set by the wall conductivity, the
input/output coupling and any dielectric loss; the corresponding
linewidth is $\kappa=\omega/Q_L$.
\end{statement}

\paragraph{Form factor and effective volume.}
For a haloscope mode of profile $\bm E_{nml}(\bm r)$ in a static
field $\bm B_0\,\hat z$, the dimensionless form factor that enters
the Sikivie signal-power formula \cref{eq:axion-power} is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cavity-form-factor}
C_{nml} \;=\; \frac{\bigl|\!\int\!\dd^3 r\;\bm E_{nml}\!\cdot\!\hat z\bigr|^2}
{V\,\int\!\dd^3 r\;|\bm E_{nml}|^2},
\end{equation}
maximised by modes whose electric field is parallel to $\bm B_0$
over the entire mode volume $V$ (the TM$_{010}$ mode of a
cylindrical cavity, $C_{010}\approx 0.69$). For dark-photon
absorption the same mode quantities appear, with the form factor
weighted by the dark-photon polarisation rather than by $\bm B_0$.

\section{Single-microwave-photon detectors}
\label{sec:single-photon}

\begin{phenomenon}
At the noise floor of a quantum-limited heterodyne microwave
amplifier, $\sim 1\,\text{photon}/\sqrt{\text{Hz}}/\text{mode}$,
the relevant question for a haloscope is no longer the analog
power level but the discrete arrival of single photons in the
cavity. A transmon coupled to the cavity provides exactly this
single-photon counting capability, and three families of
implementations have been demonstrated.
\end{phenomenon}

The three implementations differ in whether they operate on
\emph{intracavity} photons (counting the steady-state mode
population) or on \emph{itinerant} photons (counting photons that
pass through a one-port or two-port device).

\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Dispersive-shift readout}~\cite{schuster2007}: a
        transmon coupled to a microwave cavity in the dispersive
        regime, $\Delta=\omega_q-\omega_c\gg g$, has its
        frequency shifted by $2\chi$ per intracavity photon, where
        $\chi=g^2/\Delta$ is the dispersive shift. Resolving
        these shifts in qubit spectroscopy gives photon-number-resolved
        readout of the cavity (\cref{sec:disp-photon-counting}
        below).
  \item \emph{Lambda-system QND counters}~\cite{inomata2016,kono2018}:
        an artificial $\Lambda$-type three-level system arranged
        so that an incoming photon drives the qubit irreversibly
        from a ground to an auxiliary state, where it is read out.
        Used for itinerant photons (Inomata~et~al., Kono~et~al.).
  \item \emph{Direct QND single-shot detection}~\cite{besse2018}:
        a cavity-coupled transmon operates as a $\sigma_z$
        measurement on the photon-number basis, with single-shot
        fidelity sufficient to register an itinerant photon as a
        click. Demonstrated by Besse~et~al.\ in the Wallraff group.
  \item \emph{Irreversible four-wave-mixing photon counters}~\cite{lescanne2020}:
        a JJ nonlinearity engineered to give an irreversible
        $\hat a^\dagger\,\hat\sigma_-$ coupling between a signal
        photon and a transmon excitation. Demonstrated by
        Lescanne~et~al.\ in the Quantronics group; works for
        itinerant signal photons in a wide bandwidth
        (\cref{sec:fwm-photon-counting} below).
  \item \emph{Cavity-coupled transmon for dark matter}~\cite{dixit2021}:
        Dixit~et~al.\ showed the first application of a
        photon-number-resolving transmon to a dark-matter haloscope,
        with photon-counting efficiency $\eta\sim 0.5$ and
        dark-count rate $\Gamma_{\rm dc}\sim 1\,\text{mHz}$.
\end{itemize}

The two architectures developed in detail below ---
dispersive-shift readout and four-wave-mixing irreversible
coupling --- represent the two complementary routes to
single-photon sensitivity in cQED, and are the schemes adopted by
present and proposed haloscope front ends.

\section{Dispersive-shift photon counting}
\label{sec:disp-photon-counting}

\begin{phenomenon}
A transmon coupled to a microwave cavity in the dispersive regime
has its transition frequency shifted by an integer multiple of
the dispersive shift per intracavity photon. Photon-number
resolution follows from spectroscopy on the qubit at frequencies
$\omega_q + 2\chi n$ for each non-negative integer $n$.
\end{phenomenon}

\begin{statement}[Dispersive Hamiltonian]
\label{stmt:disp-H-smpd}
A Jaynes--Cummings interaction
\begin{equation}
\op{H}_{\rm JC} \;=\; \hbar\omega_c\,\hat a^\dagger\hat a
\,+\, \tfrac{\hbar\omega_q}{2}\,\hat\sigma_z
\,+\, \hbar g\,(\hat a\,\hat\sigma_+ + \hat a^\dagger\,\hat\sigma_-),
\end{equation}
in the dispersive limit $|\Delta|=|\omega_q-\omega_c|\gg g$, is
unitarily equivalent (to leading order in $g/\Delta$) to the
\emph{dispersive Hamiltonian}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:disp-H-smpd}
\op{H}_{\rm disp} \;=\; \hbar\omega_c\,\hat a^\dagger\hat a
\,+\, \tfrac{\hbar(\omega_q+\chi)}{2}\,\hat\sigma_z
\,+\, \hbar\chi\,\hat\sigma_z\,\hat a^\dagger\hat a,
\qquad \chi=\frac{g^2}{\Delta}.
\end{equation}
The qubit's transition frequency depends on the photon number
$n=\langle\hat a^\dagger\hat a\rangle$ as
$\omega_q^{(n)}=\omega_q+(2n+1)\chi$, so the qubit spectrum
\emph{is} the photon-number distribution.
\end{statement}

\paragraph{Derivation sketch.}
Apply the Schrieffer--Wolff transformation
$\op{U}=\exp[\,(g/\Delta)(\hat a\,\hat\sigma_+ - \hat a^\dagger\,\hat\sigma_-)\,]$
to the JC Hamiltonian and expand to second order in $g/\Delta$.
The off-diagonal cross-coupling cancels at first order, and the
remaining second-order term is the $\sigma_z\,\hat a^\dagger\hat a$
operator with prefactor $g^2/\Delta$, plus an irrelevant Lamb-style
shift to the qubit frequency. Schuster~et~al.~\cite{schuster2007}
demonstrated photon-number-resolved spectroscopy on a transmon
following this procedure, observing comb-like qubit-spectroscopy
peaks at $\omega_q + 2\chi n$ corresponding to photon numbers up
to $n\sim 5$.

\begin{statement}[Photon-number resolution requirement]
\label{stmt:disp-resolution}
Number-resolved readout requires the per-photon shift to exceed
both the cavity linewidth $\kappa$ (photon lifetime) and the
qubit dephasing rate $1/T_2$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:photon-resolution}
2\,\chi \;>\; \kappa,\qquad
2\,\chi \;>\; 1/T_2.
\end{equation}
With $\chi/2\pi\sim 1\,\text{MHz}$, $\kappa/2\pi\sim 100\,\text{kHz}$
and $T_2\sim 50\,\mu\text{s}$, both conditions are comfortably
satisfied in present cQED hardware.
\end{statement}

\paragraph{Use as a photon counter.}
Drive the qubit at the spectroscopy frequency $\omega_q^{(0)}$
that selects $n=0$. A measurable Rabi response indicates the cavity
is in the vacuum; the absence of a response indicates a non-zero
photon population, and the population can then be resolved by
sweeping through $n=1,2,\dots$ The Dixit~et~al.\
haloscope~\cite{dixit2021} uses this protocol with the qubit gated
on a known time window, achieving an effective dark-count rate
$\Gamma_{\rm dc}\sim 1\,\text{mHz}$ that is set primarily by
spurious thermal photons reaching the cavity from the readout chain.

\section{Four-wave-mixing photon counting}
\label{sec:fwm-photon-counting}

\begin{phenomenon}
A Josephson junction is intrinsically nonlinear: its potential
$-E_J\cos\hat\varphi$ contains a $\hat\varphi^4$ term that, in
second quantisation, generates four-wave mixing between the modes
of the circuit. Engineered correctly, this nonlinearity converts
an incoming signal photon into a qubit excitation in an
\emph{irreversible} way --- the click of a single-microwave-photon
counter for itinerant photons.
\end{phenomenon}

\begin{statement}[Josephson nonlinearity in second quantisation]
\label{stmt:josephson-fwm}
Expanding the Josephson Hamiltonian around the equilibrium phase
$\hat\varphi=0$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:josephson-expansion}
\op{H}_J \;=\; -E_J\cos\hat\varphi
\;\approx\; -E_J + \frac{E_J}{2}\,\hat\varphi^2
\,-\, \frac{E_J}{24}\,\hat\varphi^4
\,+\, \mathcal O(\hat\varphi^6),
\end{equation}
where the quadratic term combines with the capacitive energy to
form the linear LC oscillator (the qubit) and the $\hat\varphi^4$
term is the leading nonlinearity. Writing
$\hat\varphi=\sum_i \varphi_{{\rm zpf},i}\,(\hat a_i+\hat a_i^\dagger)$
as a sum over circuit modes (signal, qubit, ancilla), the
$\hat\varphi^4$ term contains four-wave-mixing products
$\hat a_i^\dagger \hat a_j^\dagger \hat a_k \hat a_l$, with the
selection rules controlled by the engineered mode frequencies
and zero-point fluctuations $\varphi_{{\rm zpf}}$.
\end{statement}

\paragraph{Engineering the irreversible coupling.}
The Quantronics scheme of Lescanne~et~al.~\cite{lescanne2020} uses
three modes:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a \emph{signal} mode (the incoming itinerant photon to
        be detected, frequency $\omega_s$),
  \item a \emph{qubit/buffer} mode (transmon, frequency $\omega_q$),
  \item a \emph{drive} mode (an external pump at frequency
        $\omega_d$).
\end{itemize}
Choosing $\omega_s + \omega_d = \omega_q + \omega_e$ for an
auxiliary excited state $|e\rangle$ above the qubit, the
relevant four-wave-mixing process becomes
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fwm-process}
\op{H}_{\rm FWM}^{(\rm res)}
\;\propto\; g_4\,\bigl(\hat a_s\,\hat a_d\,|e\rangle\langle g| + \hbox{h.c.}\bigr),
\end{equation}
in which a signal photon plus a drive photon together excite the
qubit from $|g\rangle$ to $|e\rangle$. The drive is then
adiabatically removed; the qubit decay $|e\rangle\to|g\rangle$ is
read out conventionally, leaving the signal-photon information
imprinted as a click. The \emph{irreversibility} comes from the
qubit decay channel: once the qubit decays from $|e\rangle$, the
signal photon is gone, so the detector is one-shot, not
demolition-free. This is the key innovation that makes the
\cref{eq:fwm-process} sensitive to single itinerant photons of
arbitrary signal-mode amplitude.

\begin{statement}[Detection efficiency and bandwidth]
\label{stmt:fwm-efficiency}
For a four-wave-mixing photon counter operating at signal
frequency $\omega_s/2\pi$, with mode-coupling rate
$\kappa_s\sim 1\,\text{MHz}$ to the signal port, drive amplitude
chosen to give an irreversible-coupling rate
$\gamma\sim 1\,\text{MHz}$, and qubit measurement fidelity
$\eta_{\rm meas}\sim 0.95$, the achievable single-photon
detection efficiency is~\cite{lescanne2020}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fwm-efficiency}
\eta \;\sim\; \eta_{\rm meas}\,\frac{4\,\kappa_s\,\gamma}{(\kappa_s+\gamma)^2}\,,
\end{equation}
maximised at $\gamma=\kappa_s$ to give $\eta\sim 0.5$, with
detection bandwidth set by $\kappa_s$. The dark-count rate is
limited by spurious drive-induced excitations of the qubit and
is reported in the few-mHz range.
\end{statement}

\section{Worked example: from circuit Hamiltonian to engineered Lindbladian}
\label{sec:lescanne-derivation}

\begin{example}[\textbf{Deriving the Lescanne single-microwave-photon detector}]
\label{ex:lescanne-smpd}
The high-level statements of \cref{stmt:josephson-fwm,stmt:fwm-efficiency}
hide a substantial calculation: how, exactly, does the bare $\hat\varphi^4$
Josephson nonlinearity together with a strong off-resonant pump engineer
an irreversible, non-local, non-linear Lindblad operator
$\op L=\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm nl}}\,\hat b\,\hat\sigma^\dagger$ that converts an
incoming buffer photon into a qubit excitation? This worked example
walks through the derivation of \cite{lescanne2020} step by step --- a
displacement transformation to absorb the strong pump into the frame, a
rotating-wave bookkeeping over the eight-letter expansion of $\hat\varphi'^4$
to identify the resonant four-wave-mixing term, and an adiabatic
elimination of a fast-decaying ``waste'' mode --- and is intended as the
template for the cQED-engineered-dissipator calculations that recur in
photon-counter design and (more broadly) in cat-code and pair-cat-code
stabilisation.
\end{example}

\paragraph{Setup: three modes and a junction.}
Three linear electromagnetic modes share a single Josephson junction:
the \emph{buffer} $\hat b$ (the mode where the itinerant signal photon
lands), the \emph{waste} $\hat w$ (a strongly damped ancillary mode
whose role will become clear), and the \emph{qubit} $\hat q$ (the
mode living on the junction itself, which we will eventually truncate
to a two-level system). A strong classical microwave drive --- the
\emph{pump} --- is applied to the qubit mode at frequency $\omega_p$.
The full Hamiltonian is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lescanne-Htot}
\op H/\hbar
\;=\; \sum_{m=b,w,q}\!\omega_m\,\hat m^\dagger\hat m
\;-\; \frac{E_J}{\hbar}\bigl[\cos\hat\varphi + \tfrac{1}{2}\hat\varphi^{\,2}\bigr]
\;+\; 2\epsilon_p\cos(\omega_p t)\,(\hat q+\hat q^\dagger),
\end{equation}
with the phase across the junction
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lescanne-phi}
\hat\varphi \;=\; \sum_{m=b,w,q}\varphi_m\,(\hat m+\hat m^\dagger),
\end{equation}
and zero-point phase fluctuations $\varphi_m$ set by the
circuit-design distribution of the junction over the three modes
($\varphi_q$ is the largest because the qubit lives on the junction).
The $+\hat\varphi^2/2$ counterterm subtracts the inductive piece of
the Josephson cosine that has already been folded into the linear
mode frequencies $\omega_m$ during quantisation, so what remains in
the bracket is purely quartic and higher:
$\cos\hat\varphi+\tfrac{1}{2}\hat\varphi^{\,2}=1+\hat\varphi^4/24
-\hat\varphi^6/720+\cdots$. The leading nonlinearity is therefore
$-(E_J/\hbar)\,\hat\varphi^4/24$, the source of all Kerr, cross-Kerr,
and parametric-mixing terms below.

\paragraph{Displacement of the qubit by the pump.}
The pump is strong enough that $|\xi_p|^2\gtrsim 1$ photon, so
treating it perturbatively is hopeless. Instead we go to a frame in
which the qubit's classical response to the pump has been absorbed
into the frame transformation, leaving only quantum fluctuations. For
a bosonic mode $\hat q$, the displacement operator
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lescanne-Dop}
\op D(\xi) \;=\; \exp\bigl[\xi\,\hat q^\dagger - \xi^*\hat q\bigr]
\end{equation}
is unitary and satisfies the shift relation
$\op D^\dagger\hat q\op D=\hat q+\xi$, $\op D^\dagger\hat q^\dagger\op D=\hat q^\dagger+\xi^*$,
which follows from a single-step Baker--Campbell--Hausdorff
expansion. We use this passively: $\op D$ transforms operators,
not states, and the new ``$\hat q$'' will describe quantum
fluctuations on top of a classical c-number pump amplitude.

\paragraph{The fictitious term from time dependence.}
Because the pump rotates at $\omega_p$, the displacement we want is
\emph{time-dependent}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lescanne-Dt}
\op D(t) \;=\; \exp\bigl[\xi(t)\,\hat q^\dagger - \xi^*(t)\,\hat q\bigr],
\qquad \xi(t)\;=\;\xi_p\,e^{-i\omega_p t}.
\end{equation}
A general time-dependent unitary $\hat U(t)$ acting passively on the
Hamiltonian transforms it according to
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:time-dep-frame}
\op H' \;=\; \hat U^\dagger\op H\hat U
\;+\; i\hbar\,\frac{d\hat U^\dagger}{dt}\,\hat U,
\end{equation}
with the second term, the \emph{fictitious term}, generated by the
time dependence of the frame itself --- the quantum analogue of the
Coriolis force in a rotating mechanical frame. For the displacement
\cref{eq:lescanne-Dt}, an explicit BCH calculation (using
$[\hat X,\dot{\hat X}]=2i\omega_p|\xi_p|^2$, a c-number, with
$\hat X=\xi\hat q^\dagger-\xi^*\hat q$) yields
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lescanne-fictitious}
i\hbar\,\frac{d\op D^\dagger}{dt}\,\op D
\;=\; -\hbar\,\omega_p\,\bigl(\xi\,\hat q^\dagger + \xi^*\hat q\bigr)
\;-\; \hbar\,\omega_p\,|\xi_p|^2,
\end{equation}
where the c-number piece is an irrelevant energy shift.

\paragraph{Cancelling the linear pump drive.}
After the displacement, the linear-in-$\hat q^\dagger$ pieces of the
transformed Hamiltonian are
$(\omega_q\xi_p + \epsilon_p - \omega_p\xi_p)\,e^{-i\omega_p t}\,\hat q^\dagger$,
a sum of (i) the rotating-frame qubit term, (ii) the RWA-kept piece of
the drive, and (iii) the fictitious term \cref{eq:lescanne-fictitious}.
Choosing
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lescanne-xip}
\boxed{\;\xi_p \;=\; -\,\frac{\epsilon_p}{\omega_q-\omega_p}\;}
\end{equation}
zeroes them. This is the linear-response pump amplitude. The
conjugate cancellation (linear in $\hat q$) is automatic. The pump
no longer drives the qubit linearly; it now appears only as a
c-number addition to $\hat\varphi$ inside the Josephson cosine,
\begin{equation}
\hat\varphi \;\to\; \hat\varphi + \varphi_q\bigl(\xi_p\,e^{-i\omega_p t}+\xi_p^*\,e^{+i\omega_p t}\bigr),
\end{equation}
where it can mix nonlinearly with all three quantum modes. The
linear-response value \cref{eq:lescanne-xip} is exact to leading
order in $\xi_p$; cubic-and-higher corrections from the cosine
remain, but they are suppressed by $\chi_{qq}|\xi_p|^2/(\omega_q-\omega_p)$
and are negligible in the experimental regime
($\omega_q-\omega_p\sim 300$\,MHz while $\chi_{qq}|\xi_p|^2\sim 10$\,MHz).

\paragraph{Rotating frame and the eight-letter alphabet.}
Move into the interaction picture defined by
$\op H_0/\hbar = \sum_m(\omega_m-\delta_m)\hat m^\dagger\hat m$, with
free detunings $\delta_m$ to be chosen later. Each operator picks up
a phase $\hat m \to \hat m\,e^{-i(\omega_m-\delta_m)t}$. The
phase across the junction in this frame is a sum of
\emph{eight} phasors --- six from the three modes
(annihilation and creation) plus two from the c-number pump:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lescanne-phi-prime}
\hat\varphi'(t) \;=\; B+B^*+W+W^*+Q+Q^*+P+P^*,
\end{equation}
with the dictionary
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.18}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Symbol & Operator/c-number piece & Frequency \\
\midrule
$B$    & $\varphi_b\hat b\,e^{-i(\omega_b-\delta_b)t}$ & $-(\omega_b-\delta_b)$ \\
$B^*$  & $\varphi_b\hat b^\dagger\,e^{+i(\omega_b-\delta_b)t}$ & $+(\omega_b-\delta_b)$ \\
$W$    & $\varphi_w\hat w\,e^{-i(\omega_w-\delta_w)t}$ & $-(\omega_w-\delta_w)$ \\
$W^*$  & $\varphi_w\hat w^\dagger\,e^{+i(\omega_w-\delta_w)t}$ & $+(\omega_w-\delta_w)$ \\
$Q$    & $\varphi_q\hat q\,e^{-i(\omega_q-\delta_q)t}$ & $-(\omega_q-\delta_q)$ \\
$Q^*$  & $\varphi_q\hat q^\dagger\,e^{+i(\omega_q-\delta_q)t}$ & $+(\omega_q-\delta_q)$ \\
$P$    & $\varphi_q\xi_p\,e^{-i\omega_p t}$ & $-\omega_p$ \\
$P^*$  & $\varphi_q\xi_p^*\,e^{+i\omega_p t}$ & $+\omega_p$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The leading nonlinear term $-(E_J/\hbar)\,\hat\varphi'^4/24$ is then a
sum of $8^4=4096$ ordered products. Most oscillate fast and average
to zero under RWA; only those whose total exponent vanishes survive
on the timescale of the slow dynamics.

\paragraph{The 4WM resonance.}
The product $B\cdot W^*\cdot Q^*\cdot P$ has total exponent
$-(\omega_b-\delta_b)+(\omega_w-\delta_w)+(\omega_q-\delta_q)-\omega_p$,
which vanishes when the pump frequency is locked to
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lescanne-omegap}
\omega_p \;=\; (\omega_q-\delta_q)+(\omega_w-\delta_w)-(\omega_b-\delta_b).
\end{equation}
The number of orderings of four distinct letters is $4!=24$,
exactly cancelling the $1/24$ in $\hat\varphi'^4/24$. The resulting
resonant four-wave-mixing Hamiltonian is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lescanne-H4WM}
\op H_{\rm 4WM}/\hbar
\;=\; -\,\frac{E_J}{\hbar}\,\varphi_q^2\,\varphi_b\,\varphi_w\,\xi_p\,
\hat b\,\hat w^\dagger\,\hat q^\dagger
\;+\; \hbox{h.c.}
\end{equation}
Defining the three-wave coupling
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lescanne-g3}
g_3 \;\equiv\; -\,\frac{E_J}{\hbar}\,\varphi_q^2\,\varphi_b\,\varphi_w\,\xi_p,
\end{equation}
and using the cross-Kerr expressions
$\hbar\chi_{qb}=E_J\varphi_q^2\varphi_b^2$ and
$\hbar\chi_{qw}=E_J\varphi_q^2\varphi_w^2$ (also generated by
$\hat\varphi'^4/24$, with their own combinatorics), the coupling
takes the compact form
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lescanne-g3-chi}
\boxed{\;g_3 \;=\; -\,\xi_p\,\sqrt{\chi_{qb}\,\chi_{qw}}\;}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Stark shifts and the choice of detunings.}
The $PP^*\hat m^\dagger\hat m$ products of $\hat\varphi'^4$ generate
pump-induced AC Stark shifts on every mode. The free detunings
$\delta_m$ in the rotating-frame Hamiltonian were left unfixed
precisely so that they could absorb these shifts. Choosing
\begin{equation}
\delta_q \;=\; 2\chi_{qq}|\xi_p|^2,\qquad
\delta_b \;=\; \chi_{qb}|\xi_p|^2,\qquad
\delta_w \;=\; \chi_{qw}|\xi_p|^2 + \Delta,
\end{equation}
with $\Delta$ a free residual detuning on the waste mode (kept for
the optimisation below), the Stark shifts vanish in the rotating
frame and \cref{eq:lescanne-omegap} becomes the pump-frequency
matching condition
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lescanne-omegap-final}
\boxed{\;\omega_p \;=\; \omega_q+\omega_w-\omega_b - \Delta
- |\xi_p|^2\bigl(2\chi_{qq}+\chi_{qw}-\chi_{qb}\bigr)\;}
\end{equation}
The $|\xi_p|^2$-dependent slope of \cref{eq:lescanne-omegap-final}
calibrates the pump amplitude in photon-number units, and is the
experimental signature visible in Fig.~2a of \cite{lescanne2020}.

\paragraph{Truncating the qubit.}
The qubit anharmonicity $\chi_{qq}/2\pi=146$\,MHz dominates all rates
of interest ($g_3,\kappa_b,\kappa_w\sim 1$\,MHz), so transitions to
the second excited state $|f\rangle$ are off-resonant and the qubit
is well-described as a two-level system with
$\hat q\to\hat\sigma=|g\rangle\langle e|$ and
$\hat q^\dagger\to\hat\sigma^\dagger=|e\rangle\langle g|$. The
four-wave-mixing Hamiltonian \cref{eq:lescanne-H4WM} reduces to
the three-mode coherent coupling
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lescanne-Heff}
\boxed{\;\op H_{\rm eff}/\hbar
\;=\; g_3\,\hat b\,\hat\sigma^\dagger\hat w^\dagger
\;+\; g_3^*\,\hat b^\dagger\hat\sigma\,\hat w\;}
\end{equation}
in which a buffer photon is destroyed simultaneously with the
creation of one waste photon and one qubit excitation.

\paragraph{Master equation with all dissipators.}
Including dissipation on each mode,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lescanne-master}
\frac{d\rho}{dt}
\;=\; -\,\frac{i}{\hbar}\,[\op H'',\rho]
\;+\; \kappa_w\mathcal D[\hat w]\rho
\;+\; \kappa_b\mathcal D[\hat b]\rho
\;+\; \kappa_q\mathcal D[\hat\sigma]\rho
\;+\; \tfrac{\kappa_\varphi}{2}\,\mathcal D[\hat\sigma_z]\rho,
\end{equation}
with $\mathcal D[\op O]\rho=\op O\rho\op O^\dagger
-\tfrac{1}{2}\op O^\dagger\op O\rho-\tfrac{1}{2}\rho\op O^\dagger\op O$
the standard Lindblad superoperator, and the Hamiltonian
$\op H''/\hbar$ is \cref{eq:lescanne-Heff} together with the
residual cross-Kerrs and the buffer-mode self-Kerr.

\paragraph{Adiabatic elimination of the waste.}
The experimental hierarchy is
$|g_3|,\,\chi_{qb},\,\chi_{bw},\,\chi_{bb},\,\kappa_b,\,\kappa_q,\,\kappa_\varphi
\sim \delta\kappa_w$ with $\delta\ll 1$ and
$\chi_{qw}/\kappa_w,\,\Delta/\kappa_w\sim 1$: the waste mode dissipates
on a timescale faster than every other process by a factor of
$\delta^{-1}$. The waste therefore hovers very close to vacuum, and
we expand $\rho$ in its Fock basis as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lescanne-rho-ansatz}
\rho \;=\; \rho_{00}|0\rangle\!\langle 0|
\;+\; \delta\bigl(\rho_{10}|1\rangle\!\langle 0|+\rho_{01}|0\rangle\!\langle 1|\bigr)
\;+\; \delta^2\bigl(\rho_{11}|1\rangle\!\langle 1|+\rho_{20}|2\rangle\!\langle 0|+\rho_{02}|0\rangle\!\langle 2|\bigr)
\;+\; \mathcal O(\delta^3),
\end{equation}
where each $\rho_{nm}$ is an operator on the buffer-qubit Hilbert
space. Project the master equation onto the waste Fock blocks: the
$\langle 0|\cdot|0\rangle$ block evolves slowly (at order $\delta^2$),
while the $\langle 0|\cdot|1\rangle$ and $\langle 1|\cdot|1\rangle$
blocks evolve fast (at order $1$, set by the waste decay $\kappa_w$).
The off-diagonal $\rho_{01}$ and the population $\rho_{11}$ therefore
relax to a quasi-steady-state on the timescale $1/\kappa_w$, much
faster than $\rho_{00}$ changes. Setting their time derivatives to
zero and solving for them in terms of $\rho_{00}$ (the operator
ordering subtlety $[\hat{\tilde\Delta},\hat A^\dagger]=(\chi_{qw}/\kappa_w)\hat A^\dagger$,
where $\hat A=g_3\hat b\hat\sigma^\dagger/(\kappa_w\delta)$ and
$\hat{\tilde\Delta}=(\Delta-\chi_{qw}\hat\sigma^\dagger\hat\sigma-\chi_{bw}\hat b^\dagger\hat b)/\kappa_w$,
must be tracked carefully) gives
\begin{align}
\label{eq:lescanne-rho01}
\rho_{01} &\;=\; \frac{1}{1+4\bigl|\tfrac{\Delta-\chi_{qw}}{\kappa_w}\bigr|^2}
\Bigl(2i - 4\,\frac{\Delta-\chi_{qw}}{\kappa_w}\Bigr)\,\rho_{00}\,\hat A^\dagger,\\
\label{eq:lescanne-rho11}
\rho_{11} &\;=\; \frac{4}{1+4\bigl(\tfrac{\Delta-\chi_{qw}}{\kappa_w}\bigr)^2}\,\hat A\,\rho_{00}\,\hat A^\dagger.
\end{align}

\paragraph{Effective rates.}
Define
\begin{align}
\label{eq:lescanne-knl}
\kappa_{\rm nl} &\;\equiv\; \frac{4|g_3|^2/\kappa_w}{1+4\bigl(\tfrac{\Delta-\chi_{qw}}{\kappa_w}\bigr)^2},\\
\label{eq:lescanne-Dnl}
\Delta_{\rm nl} &\;\equiv\; \kappa_{\rm nl}\,\cdot\,\frac{\chi_{qw}-\Delta}{\kappa_w}.
\end{align}
Substitution of \cref{eq:lescanne-rho01,eq:lescanne-rho11} into the
$\langle 0|\cdot|0\rangle$ block of the master equation gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lescanne-rho00}
\frac{d\rho_{00}}{dt}
\;=\; -\,i\,\Delta_{\rm nl}\,\bigl[\hat b^\dagger\hat b\,\hat\sigma\hat\sigma^\dagger,\,\rho_{00}\bigr]
\;+\; \kappa_{\rm nl}\,\mathcal D\bigl[\hat b\,\hat\sigma^\dagger\bigr]\rho_{00}
\;+\; \mathcal L_{qb}(\rho_{00})
\;+\; \mathcal O(\delta^3),
\end{equation}
with $\mathcal L_{qb}$ collecting the buffer-qubit-only Liouvillian
($\kappa_b\mathcal D[\hat b]$, $\kappa_q\mathcal D[\hat\sigma]$,
$(\kappa_\varphi/2)\mathcal D[\hat\sigma_z]$ and the residual
cross-Kerrs).

\paragraph{Optimal detuning and the engineered Lindbladian.}
The rate $\kappa_{\rm nl}$ is maximised, and the residual Hamiltonian
shift $\Delta_{\rm nl}$ vanishes, at the choice
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lescanne-Delta-opt}
\boxed{\;\Delta \;=\; \chi_{qw}\;}
\end{equation}
which gives the master equation
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lescanne-final-master}
\boxed{\;
\frac{d\rho_{qb}}{dt}
\;=\; \kappa_{\rm nl}\,\mathcal D\bigl[\hat b\,\hat\sigma^\dagger\bigr]\rho_{qb}
\;+\; \kappa_b\mathcal D[\hat b]\rho_{qb}
\;+\; \kappa_q\mathcal D[\hat\sigma]\rho_{qb}
\;+\; \tfrac{\kappa_\varphi}{2}\mathcal D[\hat\sigma_z]\rho_{qb}
\;}
\end{equation}
with the engineered jump operator
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lescanne-Lop}
\boxed{\;
\op L \;=\; \sqrt{\kappa_{\rm nl}}\,\hat b\,\hat\sigma^\dagger,
\qquad \kappa_{\rm nl} \;=\; \frac{4|g_3|^2}{\kappa_w}.
\;}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Physical interpretation.}
The coherent four-wave-mixing process \cref{eq:lescanne-Heff}
converts a buffer photon into a qubit excitation \emph{plus} a
waste photon, which would in principle reverse and put the photon
back. The waste mode, however, leaks at rate $\kappa_w\gg|g_3|$;
the reverse process has no time to start. The adiabatic elimination
collapses the coherent two-step
$\hat b\xrightarrow{g_3}\hat\sigma^\dagger\hat w^\dagger
\xrightarrow{\kappa_w}\hat\sigma^\dagger$
into a single irreversible step
$\hat b\xrightarrow{\kappa_{\rm nl}}\hat\sigma^\dagger$
at the Purcell-like rate $\kappa_{\rm nl}=4|g_3|^2/\kappa_w$.

The engineered Lindblad operator
$\op L=\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm nl}}\,\hat b\,\hat\sigma^\dagger$
has two features that make it the heart of the photon-counter:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Non-local.} The single jump operator acts on
        \emph{two} different modes (buffer and qubit). A standard
        single-mode Lindblad operator like $\hat b$ or $\hat\sigma$
        cannot witness the destruction of a buffer photon by
        the excitation of a qubit; only the engineered non-local
        $\hat b\,\hat\sigma^\dagger$ does.
  \item \textbf{Non-linear.} The qubit is \emph{raised}, not
        lowered, by the dissipative process: the engineered
        Lindbladian drives the qubit \emph{toward} its excited
        state, but only when there is a buffer photon to consume.
        Crucially, the natural qubit-decoherence operators
        ($\hat\sigma$ for relaxation, $\hat\sigma_z$ for
        dephasing) cannot generate the operator pattern
        $\hat b\,\hat\sigma^\dagger$ on their own, so they do
        not produce false detector clicks. This is what gives
        the Lescanne detector its record-low dark-count rate.
\end{itemize}

\section{Sensitivity for bosonic dark matter}
\label{sec:cavity-sensitivity}

\begin{phenomenon}
A high-$Q$ microwave cavity tuned to the Compton frequency
$\omega_\chi=m_\chi c^2/\hbar$ of bosonic dark matter accumulates
an intracavity photon population proportional to the dark-matter
field amplitude squared. A single-microwave-photon counter
reads this population out at the standard quantum limit set by
its dark-count rate; the resulting sensitivity to the underlying
coupling parameter ($\varepsilon$ for dark photons,
$g_{a\gamma\gamma}$ for axions) is set by the
counter's $\Gamma_{\rm dc}$, the cavity's $Q$ and form factor,
and the integration time.
\end{phenomenon}

\paragraph{Signal photon rate.}
Combining \cref{stmt:dark-photon-rate} (dark-photon absorption
rate per unit volume) with the cavity boundary conditions of
\cref{stmt:cavity-modes}, the signal photon rate inside the
cavity is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cavity-signal-rate}
\Gamma_{\rm sig}^{\rm DP} \;\sim\;
\varepsilon^2\,\frac{\rho_\chi}{m_{A'}}\,
V\,Q\,\omega_{A'}\,F_{\rm geom},
\end{equation}
with $V$ the cavity volume, $Q$ the loaded quality factor,
$\omega_{A'}=m_{A'}c^2/\hbar$, and $F_{\rm geom}\leq 1$ a
geometric factor that depends on the cavity-mode overlap with
the dark-photon polarisation. For axions, the corresponding rate
follows from the Sikivie signal-power
formula~\cref{eq:axion-power} divided by $\hbar\omega_a$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cavity-signal-rate-axion}
\Gamma_{\rm sig}^{\rm a} \;\sim\;
\frac{P_{\rm sig}}{\hbar\,\omega_a} \;=\;
\frac{\kappa\,g_{a\gamma\gamma}^2\,\rho_\chi\,B_0^2\,V\,Q_L\,C_{nml}}{\hbar\,m_a^2}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Numerical reach: dark photons.}
For a 1\,litre cavity at $\omega_{A'}/2\pi=5\,\text{GHz}$
(corresponding to $m_{A'}\approx 20\,\mu\text{eV}$), $Q=10^5$,
$\rho_\chi/m_{A'}\approx 10^{15}/$cm$^3$, and
$F_{\rm geom}\approx 0.5$:
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{\rm sig}^{\rm DP} \;\sim\; \varepsilon^2\,\cdot 10^{15}/\text{cm}^3\,\cdot
10^{-3}\,\text{m}^3\,\cdot 10^5\,\cdot 3\times 10^{10}/\text{s}
\;\sim\; 3\times 10^{27}\,\varepsilon^2\,\text{s}^{-1}.
\end{equation}
With $\Gamma_{\rm dc}=1\,\text{mHz}$ and a $10$-day integration
($\tau=8.6\times 10^5\,\text{s}$), the SNR-1 sensitivity is at
$\varepsilon\sim\sqrt{\Gamma_{\rm dc}\,\tau\,/\,(3\times 10^{27}\,\tau)}
\sim 10^{-15}$, in line with the order-of-magnitude estimate of
\cref{ex:transmon-counter}.

\paragraph{Numerical reach: axions.}
For an axion at $m_a c^2=4\,\mu\text{eV}$
($\omega_a/2\pi=1\,\text{GHz}$), $B_0=8\,\text{T}$, $V=1\,\text{L}$,
$Q_L=10^5$, $C_{010}=0.69$, $\kappa=1/2$, the signal-photon rate
from \cref{eq:cavity-signal-rate-axion} (in natural units, with
the standard SI conversion) gives the order-of-magnitude
estimate of \cref{ex:axion-power}: a few$\times 10^{-2}$ photons per
second at $g_{a\gamma\gamma}=10^{-15}\,\text{GeV}^{-1}$. A
ten-day integration gives $\sim 10^4$ signal photons but $\sim
10^3$ background, giving signal-to-background of order $10$ ---
just inside the QCD-axion floor $g_{a\gamma\gamma}\sim 10^{-15}\,\text{GeV}^{-1}$
in this mass band.

\paragraph{Comparison and outlook.}
The cavity microwave-photon-counting picture is the most
quantum-information-engineering-mature of the four cQED-based DM
mechanisms (\cref{ch:qp-detection,ch:qubit-antenna,ch:phonon-qubit-piezo,ch:microwave-absorption}):
the photon-number-resolved transmon was demonstrated in
2007~\cite{schuster2007}, the irreversible photon counter in
2020~\cite{lescanne2020}, and the first DM application in
2021~\cite{dixit2021}. Future improvements will come from larger
cavity volumes, higher Q (superconducting cavities approach
$Q\sim 10^{10}$), and squeezed-state input that beats the
standard quantum limit (HAYSTAC~\cite{haystac2021}). Together
with the complementary mass coverage of the recoil and
absorption-with-quasiparticle channels covered earlier in
Part~VI, this gives the cQED dark-matter programme its
mass-vs-coupling envelope.
