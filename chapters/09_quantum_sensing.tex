\chapter{Quantum sensing of fundamental physics with superconducting circuits}
\label{ch:quantum-sensing}

\section*{Chapter overview}

Superconducting circuits combine three properties that make them
attractive sensors for very weak signals: macroscopic quantum
coherence (single quanta of microwave energy can drive a transmon
between $\ket{0}$ and $\ket{1}$), gigahertz operating frequencies
that match the wavelength of dark-photon and axion candidates over
a broad mass range, and milli-kelvin operating temperatures that
suppress thermal backgrounds well below the candidate signal
energies. The same hardware that performs quantum information
processing therefore doubles as a single-quantum particle detector,
and a literature on cQED-based searches for dark matter, fifth
forces, and exotic feebly-interacting particles has emerged in the
last decade.

This chapter is a guided tour of that literature. We start with a
short introduction to the dark-matter problem and the two
phenomenologically relevant interaction families (nuclear-recoil and
electronic-recoil), specialise to the light, photon-mediated
candidates (sub-GeV WIMP-like dark matter, dark photons, and
axions) for which cQED is best suited, and then walk through four
sensing mechanisms that use superconducting hardware: phonon
absorption mediated by quasiparticle creation, infrared absorption
in a Josephson junction, phonon-to-qubit coupling via piezoelectric
substrates, and direct microwave absorption in a transmon. For
each mechanism we sketch the signal model and quote a sensitivity
estimate.
